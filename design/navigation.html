IB Cockpit · Design Decision 02 · Navigation

One screen. Everything derives from it.

You were right — leaving the flight deck to open a page breaks the whole premise. Five ways to fix it, all of them actually clickable below. Press the buttons in each demo and feel the difference before you pick.

What real flight decks do. Both dominant patterns agree with your instinct. The Garmin G1000 never moves its display frame: pages live in groups (MAP / WPT / AUX / NRST), and softkeys along the bottom bezel change per page with a BACK key to climb out a level. The Airbus MCDU and Boeing FMC use a fixed-size screen with line-select keys down the edges. Neither ever navigates you away from the cockpit — the bezel stays put and the content swaps underneath it.

My recommendation

Option 02, the Collapsing Deck. It is the G1000 model — permanent frame, softkey groups, content swapping in place — with the one fix the pure version needs: the windshield shrinks to a horizon strip when you are working and swells back to full when you return to Command. You get the immersion where it matters and the screen space where you need it.

Option 01 is the purist answer and I would happily build it. Option 03 is the fastest to use but the least like being in an aircraft. Option 05 is the most beautiful idea and the one I would advise against on its own — you would forget where things are — but its ⌘K palette is worth stealing into whichever you choose.

Tell me a number. Mixing is fine: "02, but keep 05's ⌘K" is a good answer.
